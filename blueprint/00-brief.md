# Brief

**Version:** v0 (provisional) · 2026-09-27

## Who
- **Rufus Potié** — running for President.
- **Hudson Biggar** — running for Vice President.
- They run as one ticket. Always present them together, President first.

## The problem
Every other campaign looks the same: bright school colors, glitter, sparkles, clip-art, cartoon mascots, script fonts, and slogans like "Your voice, your future." They try hard to appeal to students. Students don't care, and the effort shows.

Competitor tickets lean strongly feminine in their look (pinks, sparkles, handmade poster board). There's room for something calmer.

## Our position: the anti-campaign
We don't try hard. We look like we already have it handled.

- **Casual** — relaxed, never pleading. No "please vote."
- **Cool** — confident through restraint. Lots of empty space, few words.
- **Professional** — looks like a real brand or a well-made magazine, not a school project.
- **Slightly masculine** — solid, dark, grounded colors; condensed straight type; square corners. Not aggressive, not macho.

Think: a clothing brand's lookbook, a record label poster, a good architecture firm's website. Quiet and expensive-looking.

## Who we're talking to
The whole student body, especially the students who ignore campaign posters. The goal is for them to notice ours *because* it doesn't shout.

## What we're not
See `../references/REFERENCES.md` → Avoid. In short: no bright colors, no glitter/stars/confetti, no cartoon characters or clip-art, no script or bubbly fonts, no cheesy slogans or puns, no exclamation marks, no big grinning headshots in circles.

## One idea to hold onto
**Say less. Leave space.** If a piece looks empty, it's probably right.

## Open questions
- Print method: school photocopier (black and white) or a print shop (color)? The v0 blueprint works in both.
- Poster size limits and approval rules at school.
- Where the website lives.
- Final platform points (what Rufus and Hudson actually stand for).
